Acoustic streaming is probably not significant for the main drop dynamics in
this DynamicDrop case.

Current scales:
- f = 25250 Hz
- piston velocity amplitude = 1 m/s
- water drop radius = 1 mm
- air boundary-layer thickness ~14 um, so R/delta ~72
- water boundary-layer thickness ~3.6 um, so R/delta ~280

A typical streaming velocity estimate is only O(1-3 mm/s). The associated
air-side drag is around 1e-9 N, while the drop weight / levitation-force scale
is around 4e-5 N. The viscous stress scale is also much smaller than the
capillary pressure scale sigma/R ~72 Pa.

Conclusion: streaming may create weak recirculation or a slow secondary drift,
but it should not dominate the drop position, levitation balance, or deformation.
The current interFALFlow coupling includes time-averaged acoustic radiation
terms from the harmonic pressure field, not a resolved second-order viscous
streaming model.


interFALFlow verification / validation suggestions
==================================================

Validate the solver in layers. Do not start with the full levitated dynamic
drop, because acoustics, VOF, force coupling, and drop motion are all active at
the same time.

1. Verify the acoustic solver alone
-----------------------------------

Use the acoustic-only cases:
- WedgeLevitatorGorkovAlpha


Check:
- Pre/Pim field convergence with mesh refinement
- reflector radiation force convergence
- drop radiation force convergence
- Gorkov force comparison for small particles
- parallel vs serial consistency

The runSolidBallGorkovStudy script is useful because it already
compares simulated radiation force against Gorkov theory.

2. Verify VOF hydrodynamics without acoustics
---------------------------------------------

Disable acoustic forcing, for example with PISTON_U=0.

Check:
- static drop spurious currents
- drop volume conservation
- capillary oscillation frequency vs Rayleigh-Lamb theory
- falling/rising drop behavior
- surface-tension force balance

Volume conservation and spurious currents are especially important because the
acoustic force is applied near the interface.

3. Verify acoustic force insertion
----------------------------------

Before allowing large drop motion, compare force evaluation methods:
- integral of pr over the reconstructed interface
- volume force from -grad(pr)*beta
- Gorkov prediction for small radius
- net force from momentum balance

These should agree in sign, order of magnitude, and mesh trend. If not, likely
issues are the interface force projection, beta mask, or reconstruction.

4. Coupled static levitation check
----------------------------------

Place the drop near the predicted equilibrium height.

At equilibrium:

    Fy_acoustic ~= rho_l * V_drop * g

Track:
- drop center acceleration
- net acoustic force minus weight
- drop volume
- shape deformation
- spurious x/z drift

5. Dynamic drop response
------------------------

Perturb the initial drop position above and below equilibrium.

Expected trends:
- drop moves toward the acoustic equilibrium
- stronger piston amplitude gives stronger restoring force
- radius changes equilibrium and deformation
- damping depends on viscosity and numerical damping

6. Mesh and time-step convergence
---------------------------------

Compare at least three resolutions for:
- centerY(t)
- verticalAxis(t)
- horizontalAxis(t)
- drop volume
- net acoustic force
- max velocity

Also vary:
- deltaT
- mesh near the interface
- cells per acoustic wavelength
- PML thickness / sigmaMax
- number of MPI ranks

Practical acceptance targets:
- centerY within about 2-5%
- drop volume error below about 0.5-1%
- force trend monotonic under refinement
- parallel/serial difference small

7. Experimental comparison
--------------------------

For Andrade-style levitation, compare:
- equilibrium height
- drop aspect ratio
- threshold levitation amplitude
- qualitative mode shape

Do not expect perfect agreement until losses, streaming, contact effects,
thermal effects, transducer calibration, and real 3D asymmetry are included.

Recommended validation ladder:

    acoustic Helmholtz field
    -> Gorkov force for small particles
    -> static VOF drop without acoustics
    -> fixed-interface acoustic force
    -> levitation force balance
    -> dynamic drop motion
    -> experimental comparison
